Theory & Definition of Partial Derivatives

1. Formal Limit Definition

For a multivariable function $f(x,y)$, the partial derivative with respect to $x$ measures the instantaneous rate of change along the $x$-axis while keeping $y$ fixed as a constant:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x, y) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

Similarly, the partial derivative with respect to $y$ keeps $x$ fixed as a constant:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x, y) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x, y+h) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

2. Geometric Interpretation

  • Slicing Planes: The plane $y = y_0$ cuts through the 3D surface $z = f(x,y)$, producing a 2D curve $g(x)$.
  • Tangent Lines: The value $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_0, y_0)$ is the slope of the tangent line to that slice curve at $x = x_0$.
  • Tangent Plane: The two tangent lines span the unique 2D linear tangent plane that best approximates $f(x,y)$ locally around $(x_0, y_0)$.

3. Gradient Vector ($\nabla f$)

The gradient vector combines both partial derivatives into a 2D vector on the input domain:

$$\nabla f(x_0, y_0) = \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right\rangle$$

The gradient points in the direction of **steepest ascent** on the surface, and its length $|\nabla f|$ is the rate of maximum increase.
