Volumes of Known Cross Sections

We have seen how to find the volume that is swept out by an area between two curves when the area is revolved around an axis. On this page we will explore volumes where the cross section is known, but isn't generated by revolution.

Try the following:

  1. The applet initially shows the yellow region bounded by f (x) = x +1 and g(x) = x² from 0 to 1. This is the base of a solid which has square cross sections when sliced perpendicular to the x-axis (i.e., one side of each square lies in the yellow region). Move the x slider to move a representative slice about the region, noticing that the size of the square changes. The two ends are also shown in light gray. Note that the slice is sticking up from the screen, and the perspective causes it to look like a rectangle. The volume of one of these square slices with thickness dx and side length s is just the area of the square times dx, or s²dx. But s is just the distance between the two curves for a given x, or s = x +1 - x². So the integral which sums up all these slices is just . We will leave it as an exercise for the reader to show that this is 41/30 or about 1.367.
